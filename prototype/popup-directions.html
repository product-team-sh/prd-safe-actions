2. Four directions

3. Recommendation

Recommended: D, quiet irreversibility note, on a 32rem card with no header rule. It is the only direction where the one serious fact (can't be undone, and the way back) stands apart from the consequence without colour, matches the review's rule "mention can't be undone only where true", and still reads like production's Snooze: title, short body, two buttons. It borrows B's no-rule, narrower card and A's wording discipline. C is scannable but turns every popup into a list, which fights "one sentence is enough".

What breaks / what you will be challenged on. (1) The note row is new UI that Tasks' Snooze does not have; production puts "cannot be undone" inline, so engineering will ask why this is not the Snooze sentence (answer: A is the fallback and costs nothing). (2) A narrower card and no header rule are production deviations (ConfirmationModalV3 is 40rem with a rule), so shipping them means changing the shared modal or a new variant, not only these six. (3) The empty-sequence line needs the app to know "all prospects of the sequence are selected" at confirm time, which is a data condition, not copy. (4) Without a count, a user who selected 1 sees "Selected prospects", which reads slightly off; the title and bar already show the selection.

4. Copy deck under D

Rules: lead with who is affected, no count, at most 2 sentences in the body, sentence case, no em dashes. Exclusions use "will be skipped" and the plural group form ("Prospects who are already finished"), so there is no count and no is/are logic. Skipped over "excluded" because it says what happens to those prospects as a plain action (a 12-year-old reads it as "not touched"), whereas "excluded" is selection jargon and read as an error in the review. Exclusion line only appears when it applies. The note row appears only for Unsubscribe and Mark as finished.
