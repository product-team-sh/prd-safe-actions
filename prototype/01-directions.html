Seven prospects, one click, no way back

Thirteen ways to stop that happening, grouped by when they act: before the click, at the click, and after it. Each one is drawn on the real Sequence > Prospects bulk bar, the worst case from the audit, and scored on how it would carry to the other 34 places.

My recommendation is the layered set just below. The thirteen directions follow, then a comparison table.

What happened to the reported customer, 7 Oct

  1. 13:18Sequence gets its first prospects. It reaches 7.
  2. 14:08Header checkbox ticks all 7 (the page holds 25). "Select all" link is hidden because 7 of 7 is already all. One click on an unlabelled X.
  3. 14:08All 7 removed. Their events, drafts and notes in this sequence are deleted for good.
  4. 14:12Customer screenshots "Prospects 0" and files the bug.
  5. 8 OctThey add 109 new prospects. The 7 are never re-added.

Recommendation: four layers, each cheap where it can be

No single pattern fits 35 places. A dialog on everything trains people to click through; undo on everything is a backend project per action. So each layer does one job.

1. Make the risk visible

Every bulk bar, before anything is clicked

  • Selection says its true scope: "All 7 prospects in this sequence"
  • Risky actions get words, sit apart, and carry the count

Directions A, B, C UI only

2. Ask in proportion

At the click, only when it matters

  • Small and undoable: no dialog, undo toast
  • Bulk or permanent: dialog with count and what happens
  • Whole sequence, 1,000+, or account-wide: type to confirm

Direction D (the rule) using E, F, G UI only

3. Let people take it back

Seconds to days after

  • Undo for 10 seconds on bulk sequence and inbox changes
  • Restore removed prospects for 30 days, if data backs it

Directions H, I Backend

4. Guard the account

Security and money

  • Re-enter password or code to switch off MFA or SSO
  • Total credits shown before any spend
  • Plan changes name which mailboxes stop
  • Every bulk action leaves a record with who and when

Directions J, K, L, M Small backend

After the click

Make the mistake cheap

H

Undo for 10 seconds

Backend
Removed 7 prospects from "SaaS founders, Oct" Undo

The rows leave the table at once; the real removal waits 10 seconds. Bulk removal already runs as a background job, so the job just starts late and can be cancelled.

Forgiveness beats asking: "Undo > Are you sure?". Loss Aversion (#65) is answered after the fact, without slowing everyone who meant it. Peak-End Rule (#91): the moment ends in relief, not dread.
